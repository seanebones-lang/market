## Independent golden arithmetic

The accounting fixture starts with `$1,000` and buys `2 BTC @ $100` with a `$1` fee:

```text
cash after buy                 = 1,000 - 200 - 1 = 799
inventory / gross basis       = 2 BTC / $200
mark at $110                  = 799 + 2 * 110 = $1,019
costed liquidation at $109
  with $2 estimated fee       = 799 + 2 * 109 - 2 = $1,015
```

Selling `1 BTC @ $120` with a `$1.20` fee produces `$118.80` cash, allocates `$100`
weighted-average basis, and realizes `$20` gross P&L. The remaining one BTC has `$100` basis. A
`$115` mark and `$114` costed exit with `$1.14` fee produce `$1,032.80` marked equity and
`$1,030.66` NLV. Every identity residual is zero.

## Anti-look-ahead and directional execution

`future_jump.csv` produces the EMA signal at a `$12` close and sets the next bar open to `$20`.
The accepted order fills only at the `$20` next open. Event order is strictly:

```text
signal bar close -> decision accepted -> next bar open -> order eligible -> fill
```

Under the directional execution fixture at a `$100` reference open, a 20-bps full spread creates
`$99.900 / $100.100` bid/ask. Ten-bps adverse slippage produces a `$100.200100` buy and a
`$99.800100` sell. The direction cannot accidentally improve either side.

## Partial-execution proof

The lifecycle fixture sends two requested orders through four broker-like executions: two one-BTC
buys at `$100` and `$110`, then one-BTC sells at `$120` and `$90`. It proves:

- two orders remain distinct from four executions;
- each one-BTC execution is partial relative to its two-BTC parent;
- weighted-average gross basis is `$105` per BTC;
- entry fees allocate proportionally across exits;
- the exits become one winning and one losing closed trade; and
- the four executions form exactly one completed flat-to-flat round trip.

The hourly event engine deliberately does not invent partial executions from OHLC data. The
lifecycle/accounting analyzers instead accept deterministic broker-like execution streams. Live
normalization, recovery, and order-state persistence remain G6 work.

## Insufficient-cash proof

The new boundary test starts with `$15`. At the `$12` signal close, one BTC is affordable and the
risk gate accepts the request. The eligible next bar opens at `$20`, so execution rejects the full
request with `insufficient_cash_at_execution`.

The result has exactly one accepted order, one execution rejection, zero fills, zero fees, zero
inventory, zero P&L, zero closed trades, zero round trips, and zero terminal liquidations. The
accounting journal remains the single `$15` opening balance. This proves a price gap cannot create
negative cash or a hidden partial execution.

`$0.3800003800`; no side is omitted or halved.
