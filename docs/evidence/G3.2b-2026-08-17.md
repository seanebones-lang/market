## Decision

Do not build the G3.3 registry around `g3-ema-v1` protocol 1.0 and do not spend the frozen market
sample running a study that the synthetic design screen cannot pass with useful probability.
Protocol 1.0 is retired before parameter execution because its joint requirements are not a
workable discovery design under the declared costs and fold lengths.

This is a rejection of the **study design**, not a rejection or validation of EMA crossover. The
generator used no G1 price, EMA, signal, trade, P&L, candidate ranking, benchmark, or holdout
strategy output. G3 therefore remains open, and the platform still has no demonstrated trading
edge.

## Variance-normalized model

The stationary latent momentum state is:

```text
rho = 0.5 ** (1 / signal_half_life_hours)
z[t] = rho * z[t-1] + sqrt(1 - rho^2) * eta[t]
```

Hourly log return is:

```text
sigma_hour = annual_volatility / sqrt(8760)
r[t] = sigma_hour * (
    sqrt(signal_variance_fraction) * z[t]
    + sqrt(1 - signal_variance_fraction) * epsilon[t]
)
```

`eta` and `epsilon` are independent standardized Student-t innovations with five degrees of
freedom. Both have unit variance, the latent state has unconditional unit variance, and the
unconditional hourly return variance remains `sigma_hour ** 2` as the signal fraction changes.
Every path targets 60% annualized hourly volatility and uses 35,061 synthetic bars, matching the
frozen development bar count but not any G1 price.

The scenarios range from no predictable momentum to a deliberately extreme detectability stress:

| Scenario | Signal variance fraction | Half-life |
|---|---:|---:|
| Null | 0.00% | 48h (irrelevant under null) |
| Weak short momentum | 0.25% | 12h |
| Moderate short momentum | 1.00% | 12h |
| Strong short momentum | 3.00% | 12h |
| Extreme detectability stress | 7.00% | 12h |

The final scenario is an upper stress, not a claim about plausible BTC predictability. Seventeen
of its 100 paths spanned more than 100x and the maximum spanned 704.7x. Those facts are retained in
the result and make the scenario intentionally generous to detectability, not an economic base
case.

## Calibration and fail-closed checks

No final replicate failed calibration. Across scenarios:

- median realized annualized volatility was 59.88% to 60.10%;
- the full realized range was 58.55% to 64.54%, within the predeclared 8% relative tolerance;
- latent variance remained within 35% of unit variance;
- the largest absolute hourly log return was 0.4371, below the 0.50 hard bound;
- all prices were finite and positive and no path exceeded the 1,000x numerical-safety ceiling;
  and
- the largest reported per-trade standard deviation was 3,860.1 bps (38.60%), below the
  30,000-bps (300%) fail-closed bound and orders of magnitude below the invalid exploratory run.

The 100x path count is a retained interpretability diagnostic, not a calibration rejection rule.
A correctly variance-scaled multi-year random walk can accumulate a large range; conditioning it
away would select convenient paths. The separate hourly-return, volatility, finite-price, and
trade-dispersion checks guard the numerical failure that invalidated the original table.

## Power-relevant replay

Each synthetic replicate implements the parts of protocol 1.0 that are defined without market
data:

- all 36 preregistered EMA pairs and the production SMA-seeded EMA definition;
- nine frozen validation/test window lengths and 338 prior indicator-context bars;
- flat `$1,000` scoring resets with no context position or P&L carry;
- validation ranking by primary-cost mean net trade return and one following test per selected
  pair;
- next-bar-open directional spread/slippage, per-fill fees, fixed requested quantity with the
  `$150` risk resize, and costed terminal liquidation;
- primary and doubled test costs applied to the same fold selection; and
- a labeled 499-resample, five-trade moving-block lower-bound approximation.

The synthetic scorer was reconciled against the production backtest engine on deterministic paths.
Trade count, aggregate net P&L, and every closed trade's net return match under both primary and
doubled cost profiles.

Flat-price round-trip drag from the repository's multiplicative fill arithmetic is **229.54 bps**
under primary costs and **458.17 bps** under doubled costs. These are the exact counterparts to the
protocol's approximate 230/460-bps shorthand.

The following are model-based frequencies, not market estimates. “Subset pass” requires all
representable protocol criteria 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 8 in the same replicate.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Null | -218.8 | -447.6 | 181.0 | 0% | 0% | 88% | **0/100** |
| Weak short | -217.0 | -445.8 | 173.5 | 0% | 0% | 95% | **0/100** |
| Moderate short | -166.2 | -395.7 | 138.0 | 0% | 0% | 83% | **0/100** |

| Extreme stress | +154.8 | -79.1 | 95.5 | 21% | 21% | 39% | **0/100** |

For each zero-success scenario, the exact one-sided 95% binomial upper bound on the subset pass
probability is approximately 2.95%. This does not bound full-protocol power, which can only be no
greater because four additional criteria are omitted.

In the extreme stress:

- 76% passed positive primary results;
- 21% passed the +10-bps lower-bound screen;
- 21% passed positive doubled-cost results;
- 13% had at least seven positive folds;
- 39% reached 100 trades;
- 17% passed profit concentration; and
- 51% passed the represented risk limits.

Only one of 100 extreme replicates passed doubled costs and the 100-trade floor together. No
replicate passed criteria 1 through 5 together. Removing criterion 3 alone would permit one
otherwise-complete subset pass, and removing criterion 5 alone would permit a different one. This
confirms that the high-cost robustness requirement and trade-count floor bind in opposing parts of
the parameter/effect space; concentration and fold consistency add further power loss.

## Criteria deliberately excluded

This is not a counterfeit full-protocol pass calculation. Criteria 7, 9, 10, and 11 are excluded:

- market benchmarks cannot be simulated without adding a separate economic model;
- protocol 1.0 never defines how nine fold-specific selections become one final pair, so its final
  neighborhood test is not executable without invention;
- regime, declared-gap, and additional-delay stresses require choices not yet frozen; and
- the relation between 36-pair Holm candidate inference and the adaptive walk-forward selection
  rule is unresolved.

Training data also has no modeled selection role because protocol 1.0 ranks candidates on
validation mean and does not specify a separate training operation. Adding one would change the
design rather than implement it.

Because omitted graduation criteria can only reduce the chance of a full pass, the 0/100 subset
result is sufficient to reject execution of protocol 1.0 as a useful discovery study. It is not
sufficient to estimate the power of a future, fully specified successor.

## Cost-route interpretation

The review's proposed 95-bps double-count correction was not accepted. Repository code and current
official materials distinguish the v1 market-maker route, where compensation is represented in
the spread, from v2 exchange routing, where a taker fee can apply separately. `costs.py` already
models those routes separately. The material uncertainty is whether v2, the lowest-volume tier,
20-bps displayed spread, and 10-bps adverse slippage describe the intended deployment route in
practice.

Protocol 1.0's cost assumptions were not altered for this diagnostic. Measuring read-only v1/v2
estimated prices, best bid/ask, fee tier, and fee estimates remains the highest-value input to a
successor design. Such a sampler can estimate quoted/estimated costs but cannot identify realized
slippage without executions.

## Holdout disclosure and successor status

The final-holdout market direction and endpoints were disclosed before this checkpoint, as recorded
in `docs/reviews/2026-08-17-g3-study-design-review.md`. No strategy output was generated, so the
window remains strategy-unseen. It is no longer described as literally market-path blind.

Protocol 1.0 is now retired, and any successor changes costs, criteria, selection, inference, or
final-pair semantics materially. The existing final year may be retained only as a clearly labeled
strategy-unseen secondary audit. It must not be presented as an untouched confirmatory holdout for
a successor whose design was informed by its disclosed regime. A new prospective confirmatory
window requires future data.

## Requirements for protocol 2.0

Before G3.3 resumes, a prospective successor must:

1. bind the intended execution route and observed/estimated cost distribution, while keeping
   conservative stress as a separately interpreted robustness result;
2. define the role of training data, validation eligibility, zero-trade candidates, fold-specific
   selection, and one final pre-evaluation parameter decision;
3. select an estimand and sample-size rule supported by prospective power rather than combining an
   arbitrary 100-trade floor with slow trend candidates;
4. freeze the bootstrap/block unit and the inferential treatment of an adaptive 36-candidate
   pipeline, including whether candidate-wise Holm is scientifically appropriate;
5. make benchmark, neighborhood, delay, gap, regime, concentration, and risk rules executable
   without post-result judgment;
6. remain strategy-agnostic at the registry and evidence-schema layer; and
7. define the existing strategy-unseen audit and a future confirmatory window without relabeling
   either.

Until those items are complete, G3.3 is paused. G4 and all later graduation gates remain locked.
